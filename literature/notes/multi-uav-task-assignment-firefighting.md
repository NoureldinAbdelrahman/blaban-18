## Abstract (verbatim)

Forest fires represent a growing global threat, with millions of hectares lost annually due to their rapid spread and devastating impact on ecosystems, biodiversity, and human settlements. The increasing frequency and intensity of such disasters motivate the need for intelligent and automated solutions capable of responding swiftly and efficiently. In this context, cooperative task assignment for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) emerges as a promising approach to enhance aerial firefighting and surveillance operations. However, this remains a challenging problem due to its combinatorial nature, multiple conflicting objectives, and operational constraints. This work formulates the UAV–task assignment problem as a multi-objective optimization model that maximizes task value while minimizing UAV losses and the number of deployed UAVs. To solve this problem, four meta-heuristic algorithms are investigated: Simulated Annealing (SA), Genetic Algorithm (GA), Discrete Grey Wolf Optimizer (DGWO), and Binary Firefly Algorithm (BFF). Each method is adapted to operate directly on binary assignment matrices with appropriate constraint-handling mechanisms. Simulation results demonstrate that the Binary Firefly Algorithm (BFF) achieves the best overall performance in terms of solution quality and consistency, while Simulated Annealing (SA) exhibits the fastest convergence speed among the compared methods. Overall, the evaluated algorithms effectively produce high-quality feasible task allocations, though runtime trade-offs must be considered for time-critical wildfire deployment scenarios.
