## Abstract (verbatim)

In modern autonomous aerial defense, the cooperative interception of hostile threats presents a complex, dynamic optimization challenge. This paper addresses the multiagent Target-Attacker-Defender (TAD) game, where a team of defending Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) must coordinate to protect a high-value asset from non-cooperative attackers. We formulate the TAD problem as a constrained multi-objective optimization task, balancing formation cohesion, interception efficiency, and target guarding. While recent literature explores learning-based approaches, this work rigorously evaluates the efficacy of nature-inspired metaheuristic algorithms for realtime decision-making. We implement and compare four distinct solvers: Simulated Annealing (SA), Genetic Algorithm (GA), Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO), and the Bat Algorithm (BAT). Extensive simulations across balanced, aggressive, and defensive tactical scenarios reveal significant trade-offs between solution stability and optimality. Our results demonstrate that while population-based methods like GA and GWO offer superior consistency with low variance (std dev < 0.04), the Bat Algorithm achieves the highest peak performance, locating ”super-optimal” defensive configurations with a mean fitness improvement of approximately 13% over standard evolutionary methods. This study provides a unified framework for comparative analysis and offers critical insights into selecting optimization strategies for swarm-based aerial defense systems.
