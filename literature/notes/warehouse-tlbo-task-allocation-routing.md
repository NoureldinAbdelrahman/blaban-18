## Abstract (verbatim)

The coordination of autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) in modern warehouses presents a complex multiobjective optimization problem involving task allocation, routing efficiency, collision avoidance, and energy management. As warehouse systems scale, many existing optimization approaches become computationally expensive or require extensive parameter tuning. This paper proposes a Teaching–LearningBased Optimization (TLBO) framework for multi-robot task allocation and routing in structured warehouse environments. The proposed method integrates task assignment, robot routing, battery-aware operation, and collision avoidance within a unified optimization formulation. The TLBO algorithm is evaluated through simulation in three warehouse instances of increasing complexity. Its performance is compared with Genetic Algorithm (GA), Simulated Annealing (SA), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) under identical modeling assumptions. Experimental results demonstrate that TLBO achieves stable convergence behavior and consistently produces feasible collision-free solutions across all warehouse scenarios while eliminating the need for algorithm-specific parameter tuning. The results indicate that TLBO provides a robust and scalable optimization framework for energy-aware multi-robot warehouse coordination.
