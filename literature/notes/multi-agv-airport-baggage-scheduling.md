## Abstract (verbatim)

Efficient coordination of Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) is critical for mitigating the multi-billion dollar operational losses associated with airport baggage mishandling. This paper presents a multi-objective optimization framework for AGV scheduling, formulated as a Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (VRPTW) on a bidirectional linear topology. We evaluate five distinct optimization paradigms: three established meta-heuristics (Simulated Annealing (SA), Genetic Algorithm (GA), and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)), a proposed Hybrid Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) algorithm, and a Reinforcement Learning (RL) (Q-Learning) approach. The Hybrid ABC approach utilizes discrete neighborhood operators for routing and continuous vector updates for temporal scheduling, while the Q-Learning agent offers sub-second responsiveness for dynamic environments. Experiments across three operational scenarios (standard, small, and a high-pressure First Come First Serve (FCFS) instance) demonstrate that the Hybrid ABC algorithm achieves the highest optimality, improving upon the traditional FCFS method by 61% and reducing fleet requirements by 40% in the Standard instance. Conversely, the Q-Learning agent provides the highest execution efficiency, establishing a clear trade-off between planning quality and real-time responsiveness.
